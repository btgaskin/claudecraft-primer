# Exploratory methods

Adapted from the [Tradecraft Primer](https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/static/Tradecraft-Primer-apr09.pdf), printed pages 27–36.

## Brainstorming — pp. 27–29

Use to broaden the possible explanations, variables, or approaches before settling on a frame.

- Pose a clear focal question.
- Generate varied possibilities before evaluating them. Include perspectives outside the immediate domain when useful.
- Group related ideas, preserve potentially informative outliers, and identify the assumptions behind them.
- Evaluate relevance, plausibility, evidence, and investigative value. Select the ideas that deserve development.

Keep generation and evaluation as distinct phases. For an assistant working alone, describe the result as generated possibilities; do not imply that a diverse group participated. Human workshop timing and voting rules from the primer are optional facilitation details.

## Outside-In Thinking — p. 30

Use when a question may be framed too narrowly around familiar or controllable factors.

- Describe the issue in broader terms.
- Consider relevant social, technological, economic, environmental, and political forces beyond the focal actor's control.
- Examine actors and factors that can be influenced more directly.
- Trace plausible mechanisms from external forces to the focal issue, then check the available evidence for those links.

Present only forces with a useful connection to the question. The category list is a prompt for discovery, not a mandatory report structure.

## Red Team Analysis / Actor Perspective — pp. 31–33

Use when understanding another actor's response requires examining their circumstances and interpretation of events.

- Establish what is known about the actor's information, goals, constraints, incentives, relationships, and decision process.
- Distinguish documented preferences from inferred motives and unresolved possibilities.
- Consider how the actor could interpret the same event differently, including internal disagreement or limited control.
- Develop plausible responses and identify evidence that would distinguish them.

First-person sketches are optional and must be labeled as hypothetical. Keep material uncertainty visible. Ground individual claims in relevant evidence; demographic or cultural labels alone do not establish motives. This adaptation uses the method for stakeholders generally and does not assume an adversarial relationship.

## Alternative Futures Analysis — pp. 34–36

Use when interacting uncertainties make several futures useful for planning.

- Define the focal question and time horizon.
- Identify the major drivers and consequential uncertainties.
- Select a small set of distinct, coherent scenarios. A two-axis matrix can help when two sufficiently distinct uncertainties organize the problem well.
- Describe the conditions and mechanisms leading to each scenario, including constraints and warning signs.
- Compare how proposed decisions fare across the scenarios and identify options that remain useful under several outcomes.

Do not force the problem into four quadrants when the chosen axes produce redundant or incoherent worlds. Scenarios illustrate conditional possibilities; assess their likelihood separately when the task requires it. Preserve uncertainty about factors the scenarios omit.
